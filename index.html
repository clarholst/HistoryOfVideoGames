Week 6

Mind's Eclipse

Assignment: Design a mobile game concept around a chosen class theme. This project presents a time-loop narrative focused on life in Japan and cognitive/emotional consequences. It connects to the core game loop, monetization reflection, and ethical concerns in game design.

Read more

Ideation

This game is based on one of several game development ideas I have been working on. It was initially thought of as a PC game, but the limitations of the phone screen and the natural interruption and resumption of mobile play might actually suit it well.

I really like emotional narrative games (Omori), time-loops (Outer Wilds or Until Dawn), and choice-driven consequences (Undertale), which shaped this concept. The time-loop mechanic can also fit mobile sessions, which are often short.

Game Name

心の蝕 (Kokoro no Shoku), or Mind's Eclipse.

Meaning: Kokoro (心) means mind, heart, or inner self. Shoku (蝕) means eclipse or erosion. It also carries a sense of being gradually eaten away, which fits the deterioration mechanic.

Core Gameplay

You play a character who wakes in the same small apartment in Japan every day. Each loop lasts about 8 to 12 minutes and covers one evening. You explore a tiny space: apartment, corridor, and convenience store. You talk to Haruki, a neighbor who feels vaguely familiar. You make small choices. Then the loop ends and starts again.

The game never directly says it is measuring behavior. Consequences accumulate quietly: a neglected plant dies, graffiti starts reflecting your choices, and the atmosphere shifts. Your mental state appears visually: text flickers, colors desaturate, and dialogue coherence degrades after repeated harmful choices or deaths. The goal is to break the loop by observing patterns and understanding cause and effect.

Monetization Strategy

One-time purchase around 3 to 4 euros. No ads, no in-app purchases, no energy systems, and no artificial premium pain points. The full game is available from the start. The experience is intentionally short and complete, not endless.

Ethical Concerns

The biggest concern is mental-health representation. Dissociation, paranoia, and psychological breakdown should be treated with care and informed by credible cognitive-science or clinical perspectives. Content warnings are necessary, and negative routes should never glorify harmful behavior.

A second concern is using knowledge about guilt, memory, and perception to affect players emotionally. The design should challenge and unsettle without harming the player. External feedback from mental-health experts is important before release.
